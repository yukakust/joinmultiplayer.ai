# Ethics

The laboratory is open. Evidence still needs boundaries.

## Minimize harm

- Do not expose another person's private information, secrets, credentials, or
  restricted records.
- Do not publish material that creates an avoidable, concrete risk to a person.
- Do not ask contributors to break professional duties or confidentiality.
- Do not treat a model answer or a laboratory result as medical, legal,
  financial, or safety-critical advice.

## Preserve agency

- Participation is voluntary.
- Publication permission must be explicit.
- Credit may use a name, a handle, or remain anonymous.
- A contributor may ask to remove their contribution from the current project
  view, although public Git history, forks, and third-party archives may remain.

## Preserve the record

- Never rewrite an observation to make it cleaner.
- Separate raw material, interpretation, and verdict.
- Publish negative and inconclusive results.
- Record corrections without pretending the original mistake never happened.
- Expertise is evidence to investigate, not authority that ends a question.

## Keep the claim narrow

One case proves one case. A self-selected online sample does not represent
humanity. A model comparison made today does not automatically describe the
same products tomorrow.

The project belongs to the people who participate in it. These rules protect
their evidence and agency; they do not prescribe how a decentralized movement
must express itself.
